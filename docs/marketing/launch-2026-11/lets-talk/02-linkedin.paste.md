Most hard conversations at work get rehearsed once, in the shower, and then said for real.

Asking for a raise. Telling a manager a deadline won't hold. Giving a teammate feedback they won't like. We go in with a script in our heads, the other person says something we didn't expect, and the script falls apart.

I make small apps on my own, and one of them is a practice room for exactly that. You pick a scene, or describe your own, and talk it through with an AI character who has a mood and doesn't give in on the first try. A meter shows when the conversation heats up or cools down, and after each turn a coach note says what landed and what stung.

The structure underneath is Nonviolent Communication, the framework mediators use: say what happened, how you feel, what you need, and make a request the other person can say yes or no to. It sounds obvious written down. It is much harder with someone pushing back, which is why practising it helps.

It runs in the browser, and you can try it as a guest without an account. It is a practice tool, not therapy.
