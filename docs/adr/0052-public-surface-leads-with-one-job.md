# 0052. The public surface leads with one job; the operator cockpit stays behind the operator account

Status: Accepted 2026-10-27 (owner approved after the critical audit). Date: 2026-10-27

Related: [`VISION.md`](../../VISION.md) §1 and §2.1, ADR
[0016](0016-mushi-as-the-app-recipe-control-plane.md) and
[0017](0017-strike-the-plan-019-020-sequencing-gate.md) (the recipe and
portfolio features that never lead a public surface), `apps/docs/content/index.mdx`,
`apps/docs/lib/landing-copy.ts`, `apps/docs/content/demo/diagnosis.mdx`,
`apps/docs/content/quickstart/lovable-bolt.mdx`, `README.md`.

## Context

The critical audit of 2026-10-27 measured the front door:

- No outside user has ever sent a bug report. All 3 outside installs stopped
  on localhost, and the landing recorded 0 visits in 60 days.
- The landing offered 6 or more first actions and led with a six-step
  lifecycle diagram, then CI billing, voice intake, Datadog, Bugsnag,
  Crashlytics, Teams and Discord. The README gave 3 different first commands.
- "Try the demo, no signup" meant installing an MCP server. There was nothing
  a visitor could look at.
- The Lovable page told builders who have no terminal to run `npx`, although a
  no-build script tag already existed.
- In 2026 PostHog, Replit, Sentry and Lovable all ship crash-driven AI fixing,
  mostly free and inside tools builders already use. The sharpest job left
  for Mushi is the one that starts from a person, not a crash.

## Decision

- **One job leads every public surface.** "A user said your app is broken.
  Mushi Mushi tells you why in plain English and hands Cursor, Claude Code or
  Lovable the fix." It works without Sentry or any monitoring, and the free
  plan needs no AI key of the user's own.
- **The landing has one primary action and one secondary link.** The action is
  the hero's install box: Claude Code, Cursor, Lovable or Bolt (a script tag
  pasted through a prompt), and other editors. The link is a sample diagnosis
  anyone can read with no signup and no install (`/demo/diagnosis`). Then how
  it works in three steps, proof (cost, no monitoring, you approve the fix)
  and the FAQ.
- **Everything else moves off the landing, not out of the docs.** The bug
  lifecycle, integrations and the mediator map, the Sentry comparison, the CI
  cost summary, the console tour and self-hosting live on docs pages linked
  from one "More" line.
- **The README's first screen matches.** The same sentence, one install
  command (`npx mushi-mushi`), one link to the sample diagnosis. The editor
  connection and the skills install sit below the fold.
- **The sample is honest.** It is labelled "Sample" wherever it renders. The
  report and diagnosis are written by the team in the shape Mushi stores. The
  fix prompt is generated by the console's own builder and a test fails if it
  drifts. A real model-written diagnosis appears under it, marked as one.
- **The operator cockpit stays behind the operator account.** Release trains,
  local workers, credits and spend-down, CI cost and portfolio views are never
  on the public path: not in the landing, the README's first screen or the
  quickstarts.
- **Unchanged.** The tagline ("Your AI wrote it. Mushi Mushi tells you why it
  broke."), the north-star sentence, the category line and the buyer stay as
  `VISION.md` §1 states them. The one job is a compression of the north star.

## Rejected

- **A new tagline or category word.** The audit argues "mediator" is a team
  job that a solo builder does not have yet. Changing the category is a
  positioning change for the owner to make on its own; this decision only
  changes what leads.
- **Deleting the pages for integrations, voice, CI cost and rewards.** They
  serve users who already found Mushi, and search engines already index them.
- **Calling the sample "real".** A team-written report must not borrow the
  credibility of a live one.

## Consequences

- `lib/landing-cinematic.test.ts` holds the landing to one install box, one
  hero link, the section order, and the moved blocks being reachable from the
  "More" line. `lib/sample-diagnosis.test.ts` and
  `apps/admin/src/components/report-detail/fixPrompt.sample.test.ts` keep the
  sample honest and its prompt identical to the console's.
- A feature that is not the one job can still ship and be documented. It does
  not get a landing section.
- Re-adding a section to the landing for anything but the one job needs a
  superseding ADR.

## Amendment 2026-10-28 (owner decision)

The hero's one secondary link becomes the free Production Check
(`/check?src=landing-hero`: "Not ready to install? Check your live site free,
nothing to install, no sign-up"). The sample diagnosis is still linked, one
scroll down, at the end of "How it works".

Why: a recipient walk-through on 2026-10-28 found that a skeptical stranger is
asked to run a terminal command from an unknown site before seeing any proof,
and the research the owner asked for agrees that value before trust (a free
check of their own site, public live numbers, a real person behind it) is what
lowers that barrier. The owner chose this option over keeping the landing as it
was and over leading with the check. Still one primary action and one secondary
link; `lib/landing-cinematic.test.ts` holds the new pair.


## Amendment 2026-10-29 (owner decision)

The landing adds two compact sections after "How it works": **Who it's for**
(three cards: solo builders using AI, developers who own their code, teams;
each links to its section of `/who-its-for`) and **More than bug reports** (six
cards from `/what-it-does`: first-time user test, a second AI checks every fix,
live-site watch, known security holes, the CI bill, playbooks in rounds). Each
card is a name and one line of pain; the detail stays on the two docs pages.
The hero, its install box (still the one primary action) and its one secondary
link are unchanged.

Why: the owner asked to sell the checks that find bugs nobody reports, and to
segment the audience, in plain words, and told the agent to decide the landing
after looking at strong open-source sites. Plausible's landing (hero, then a
feature grid, proof, pricing) and Supabase's (a products grid, then what each
is for) both show the product's range under a one-line hero. On the owner's
own apps, 1,068 bugs came from their own checks and 32 from people in 60 days
(`/proof`), so leaving those checks off the front page undersold what the
product does. This reverses the part of the decision that moved "CI cost" off
the landing; the bug lifecycle, integrations map, Sentry comparison, console
tour and self-hosting stay on docs pages linked from the "More" line.
