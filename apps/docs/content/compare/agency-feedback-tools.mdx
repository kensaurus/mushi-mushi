# Marker.io, BugHerd and Userback vs Mushi Mushi for agencies

> **Never used any of these?** You don't need them to use Mushi Mushi. [See what Mushi Mushi does for client apps](/use-cases/agency-client-apps) instead.

If you build sites or apps for clients, you have probably looked at Marker.io,
BugHerd or Userback. They are built for one job: a client reviews what you
built, clicks on the page and leaves a comment, and you work through the list.

Mushi Mushi is built for a different moment. The app is live, and the people
using it hit bugs your client never sees. They tap a report button, and you get
the likely cause in plain English with a prompt that fixes it.

So this is not a like-for-like comparison, and on several things an agency
cares about, the other three are ahead. This page says where.

## What the other three have that Mushi Mushi does not

- **Free seats for clients.** All three let any number of clients send and
  follow feedback without a paid seat. In Mushi Mushi a client who signs in
  takes a seat.
- **Access to one project only.** Marker.io and BugHerd invite a guest to a
  project, and Userback roles can grant access to specific projects. In
  Mushi Mushi a member of a team can read every app in that team, so a client
  needs [a team of their own](/use-cases/agency-client-apps#who-can-see-what).
- **A board the client can follow.** BugHerd has a guest task board and
  Userback a kanban board. Mushi Mushi has a list with filters.
- **Comments the client cannot see.** Marker.io, BugHerd and Userback each have
  them on some plan. Mushi Mushi does not.
- **Video.** BugHerd takes video feedback, and Marker.io and Userback attach a
  session replay on their higher plans. A Mushi Mushi report is a screenshot
  and a timeline of what the person did.
- **A SOC 2 report.** Marker.io lists SOC 2 Type II on every plan. Mushi Mushi
  has no SOC 2 report.

If collecting client feedback during a build is the job, pick one of them.

## What Mushi Mushi has that they do not

- **A written cause for each bug.** Every report comes with a plain-English
  diagnosis and a fix prompt for Cursor, Claude Code, Lovable or Bolt. The
  other three hand an AI agent the feedback over MCP; none of their pricing
  pages lists a written diagnosis. A diagnosis is an AI's reading and can be
  wrong, so check it before you ship.
- **Console and network logs on the free plan.** Marker.io includes them from
  its Team plan and Userback from Business.
- **Phone apps on the free plan.** React Native and Capacitor SDKs, on every
  plan. Userback's mobile SDK is on Business Plus.
- **No vendor mark from $29.** Builder and Studio show no Mushi Mushi mark on
  the widget.
- **You can run it yourself.** The SDKs are MIT and the server is AGPLv3.

## Using both

This is the common case. Keep Marker.io, BugHerd or Userback for client review
while you build. Add Mushi Mushi when the app goes live, for the bugs real
users hit and for the fix loop in your AI editor. Both widgets can sit on the
same site, and there is no integration between them today.

Moving off BugHerd completely? There is a
[BugHerd migration guide](/migrations/bugherd-to-mushi).

## Pick one of the other three if

- Your clients are the ones reporting, and they should not cost a seat.
- Each client must see only their own project, with no extra setup.
- You need a client-facing board, internal comments or video.
- A buyer asks for a SOC 2 report (Marker.io).

## Pick Mushi Mushi if [#pick-mushi-if]

- The reports come from the people using a live app, including phone apps.
- You want to read the likely cause first, then paste a fix prompt.
- You want console and network logs without moving up a plan.
- You want open source you can run yourself.
